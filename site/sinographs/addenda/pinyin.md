# Pinyin — how to read the voice

Every character in this school carries its modern Mandarin reading
in **pinyin** — the official romanization of Chinese, adopted in
1958 and used today everywhere from Beijing classrooms to your
phone's keyboard.[^scheme] Pinyin uses familiar letters, but about
a third of them do not say what an English reader expects. This
page is the key: read it once, come back when a syllable
surprises you.

## The tones

A Mandarin syllable has a **tone** — a fixed pitch shape that is
as much a part of the word as its consonants. Same syllable,
different tone, different word: *mā* is "mother," *mǎ* is "horse."
The tone mark sits over the vowel, and its shape draws the pitch:

## Tones on the move

Two adjustments happen in real speech, and you will hear them the
moment you listen to any recording:

- **Two third tones in a row:** the first becomes a second tone.
  "Hello," written <span class="translit pinyin">nǐ hǎo</span>, is
  said *ní hǎo*.
- **不 "not" and 一 "one"** shift tone by what follows them — 不
  is *bù* alone but *bú* before a fourth tone; 一 is *yī* in
  counting, *yí* or *yì* in the flow of speech.

Texts in this school always print the **base tone** — the
dictionary's form — and let your ear make the adjustments, which
is the standard practice.

## A modern voice for old texts

One honest reminder from
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/00-orientation/' | relative_url }}">the course orientation</a>:
pinyin gives you the *modern Mandarin* readings — the voice the
field itself uses to read the classics aloud. The classics were
not pronounced this way; the old sounds are a reconstruction
problem, and this school tells that story in its own later
chapter rather than pretending the problem away.

The audio is synthesized: one standard modern-Mandarin voice for
every syllable and every voiced line in the school, and every
clip is machine-checked before it ships — it must sing the tone
it prints, at matched loudness, or it is refused and remade.
Engine, voice, and per-file records in
<a href="{{ '/assets/audio/pinyin/CREDITS.txt' | relative_url }}">the credits file</a>,
served from this site, never hotlinked.

    Standard Chinese follow San Duanmu, *The Phonology of Standard
    Chinese* (2nd ed., 2007).

[^ipa]: The International Phonetic Alphabet — the field's standard
    notation in which one symbol always means one sound; brackets
    here give the exact target where English approximations blur.
