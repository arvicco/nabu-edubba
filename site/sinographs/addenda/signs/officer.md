## Where it comes from

The identification is honestly debated: an axe blade set
upright is the strongest modern reading; the Shuowen instead
parses "one who serves, from one 一 through ten 十." Whatever
the picture, the word is clear — the lowest rung of the old
nobility, the man-at-arms, remade by the philosophers into the
scholar who serves with his learning. Taught in
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/102/01-the-sentence-without-a-verb/' | relative_url }}">the verbless-sentence chapter</a>.

## How to remember it

Wide bar on top, narrow stance below — the exact opposite of 土
earth, whose wide line is the bottom one. The **officer**
stands shoulders first; the earth sits base first.
