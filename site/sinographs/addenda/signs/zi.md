## Where it comes from

An infant: big head, spread arms, legs still swaddled into a
single line. The drawing never changed its sense — but in the
classical books its greatest work is as a **title** of respect:
the Master. Taught in
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/03-the-master-says/' | relative_url }}">the master's chapter</a>,
as half of the 子曰 formula.

## How to remember it

A **child** in swaddling clothes, arms free. The same character
ends up as the honorific on the tradition's greatest teachers —
the child becomes the Master.
