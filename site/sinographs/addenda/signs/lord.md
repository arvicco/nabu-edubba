## Where it comes from

A hand holding the staff of office — the element that alone
means "to govern" — set over a mouth 口 issuing the commands:
the classic account of rulership drawn in two strokes of
authority. In the *Analects* the compound 君子, "lord's son,"
is remade into the book's hero: the person noble by conduct.
Taught in
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/102/' | relative_url }}">102 · Literary Chinese</a>.

## How to remember it

A staff in the hand, an order in the mouth: the **lord**.
