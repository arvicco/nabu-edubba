## Where it comes from

A scorpion drawn whole — the classic account: pincers at the
top, body banded, the tail's sting curling below. Why the
scorpion came to mean "ten thousand" no early text explains; the
loan was already ancient when writing settled, and the creature
got a new graph of its own. As a number it means less a count
than a totality: 萬物, the ten thousand things; 萬世, ten
thousand generations — forever. Taught in
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/102/03-having-and-lacking/' | relative_url }}">the having-and-lacking chapter</a>.

## How to remember it

A **scorpion** with countless legs and a curled tail — count
them all: ten thousand.
