## Where it comes from

A hand with a short tick at the wrist — the received account:
the mark sits one thumb-width below the palm, the spot where a
physician takes the pulse (the 寸口, "inch opening"). That
thumb-width became the script's smallest unit of measure, and
the character went on to mean any tiny amount — an inch of
ground, an inch of time. Taught in
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/102/04-counting-the-world/' | relative_url }}">the counting chapter</a>,
where the numbers meet their measure.

## How to remember it

Your hand 又 with one extra tick: feel for your own pulse and
your thumb lands exactly one 寸 down the wrist.
