## Where it comes from

A drawing of a beard — whiskers under a chin — borrowed whole
for the little word that joins classical clauses: "and, and
then, but." Rank five of the entire corpus; the beard's meaning
is long gone, the grammar is everywhere. Taught in <a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/06-knowing-and-walking/' | relative_url }}">the knowing-and-walking chapter</a>.

## How to remember it

Strokes hang off it like whiskers — but read **and-then**:
whatever stands on its left leads to whatever follows on its
right.
