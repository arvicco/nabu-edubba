## Where it comes from

The knife 刂 beside the cowrie 貝. On the oldest bronzes the
right-hand knife cuts into a ritual cauldron, not a shell — the
cauldron wore down to a cowrie as the graph was copied — and a
standard engraved in metal is a **rule**. The classical language
then made it a hinge: *X 則 Y*, "where X holds, Y follows, as
the rule dictates" — the twenty-third most frequent character in
the corpus, almost always in that job. Taught in
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/102/10-if-and-then/' | relative_url }}">the if-and-then chapter</a>.

## How to remember it

Cut beside the treasure: the **rule** — and "then," because the
rule says what follows.
