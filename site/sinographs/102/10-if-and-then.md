# If and then

Classical reasoning runs on one hinge: **X 則 Y** — *where X
holds, Y follows.* The hinge-character 則 is the twenty-third
most frequent in the corpus, and it is two easy pictures
standing together, so this chapter buys the pictures first:

The ancients cut their standards into bronze — the received
graph keeps a cowrie where the oldest vessels show a ritual
cauldron — and a standard cut into metal is a **rule**. From
"rule" the language took its hinge: what follows, follows *as
the rule dictates*. 八, which began life meaning "apart," gets
the knife beneath it and becomes 分, dividing.

## Duty first, books after

Confucius lists a student's obligations — behave at home, keep
your word, care widely — and only then, on this chapter's hinge:
