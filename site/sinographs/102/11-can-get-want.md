# Can, get, want

可 said *may*. This chapter fills out the row of little verbs
that sit before other verbs: 能, *is able to*; 得, *gets (to)*;
欲, *wants to*. Beside them, two verbs of bringing-into-being —
作, to make and originate; 成, to complete and become:

## What everyone wants

Confucius grants the wanting — and prices the getting:

<figure class="reading reading--script reading--stacked">

The boxes are "wealth" and "rank"</span></div>

## Able to be great — by not trying

能 and 成 share the *Laozi*'s sentence on how water-like
greatness works:

<figure class="reading reading--script">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">以其▢不自為大也，▢能成其大。</span><span class="translit pinyin">yǐ qí zhōng bù zì wéi dà yě, gù néng chéng qí dà</span><span class="gloss">"he never makes himself out to be great — and that is exactly why he can become great" — greatness as a side effect: chase the work, not the title. The boxes are "to the end" and "therefore", the second four chapters ahead; 自為 is ch7's reflexive at work</span></div>

## To make — or to pass on

作 gets the tradition's two most famous sentences about making:

  <div class="reading-lines">

    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">萬物作▢而不▢。</span><span class="translit pinyin">wàn wù zuò yān ér bù cí</span><span class="gloss">"the ten thousand things arise — and he does not refuse them" — the wise person lets the world get on with its making instead of managing it.
