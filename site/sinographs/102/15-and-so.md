# And so

Arguments need connective tissue: 故, *therefore* (grown from
"the old, the precedent" — what went before is WHY); 然, *so, 
like that* (自然, "so of itself," is nature); 從, *to follow.*
Around them, the last of the world's furniture — the earth
under heaven, and the wind that crosses it:

## The connectives at work

<figure class="reading reading--script reading--composed">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">日出，故民出。</span><span class="translit pinyin">rì chū, gù mín chū</span><span class="gloss">"the sun comes out; therefore the people go out" — the day's oldest inference, drawn since the first chapter of farming</span></div>
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">民從王，王從道。</span><span class="translit pinyin">mín cóng wáng, wáng cóng dào</span><span class="gloss">"the people follow the king; the king follows the way" — obedience is a chain, and the last link is not a person</span></div>
  </div>
  <figcaption class="citation">
    Practice lines assembled from taught characters.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

<span class="script">故</span> opens its clause and everything
after it is consequence — "and *therefore*…" — the whole
machinery of <span class="script">是以</span> in one character.
And <span class="script">從</span> is a plain transitive verb,
"follow," whose object may itself go on following: each clause
hands its object to the next as subject, the chaining you first
saw in the *Laozi*'s begettings — and will see again before
this chapter ends.

## Warm the old, and therefore

故 keeps both its lives in one famous sentence — the old, and
the hence that grows from it. The verdict you read six chapters
ago gets its reason:

<figure class="reading reading--script">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">▢故而知▢，可以為▢矣。</span><span class="translit pinyin">wēn gù ér zhī xīn, kě yǐ wéi shī yǐ</span><span class="gloss">"keep the old warm and still meet the new — such a one can serve as a teacher" — mastery is neither nostalgia nor novelty but the live wire between them. The boxes are "keep warm", "new", "teacher"</span></div>
  </div>
  <figcaption class="citation">
    <em>Analects</em> 2.11, whole at last.
    <code>urn:nabu:kanripo:KR1h0004:002:12a</code> · license: attribution.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Here <span class="script">故</span> is not "therefore" at all —
it sits in the *object* seat: warm ▢<span class="script">故</span>,
"keep *the old* warm," the noun this character grew from. Then
<span class="script">而知</span>▢, "and know the new." The
verdict you walked in <a href="{{ '/sinographs/102/09-the-art-of-asking/' | relative_url }}">the asking chapter</a> —
<span class="script">可以為</span>▢<span class="script">矣</span> —
now stands where it belongs, after its reason. One character,
two lives, and the sentence is itself the lesson it teaches:
the old (故) is exactly what a *therefore* (故) grows from.

## Heaven-and-earth, unsentimental and old

地 completes the oldest pair in the language, and the *Laozi*
uses it twice — once coldly, once kindly:

<figure class="reading reading--script reading--stacked">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">天地不仁，以萬物為▢▢。</span><span class="translit pinyin">tiān dì bù rén, yǐ wàn wù wéi chú gǒu</span><span class="gloss">"heaven and earth are not kind — they treat the ten thousand things like straw dogs" — the straw dog was a ritual figure, honored during the ceremony and discarded after: nature plays no favorites; things get their season, then the season ends. The boxes are "straw" and "dog"</span></div>
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">天長地▢。</span><span class="translit pinyin">tiān cháng dì jiǔ</span><span class="gloss">"heaven is long-lasting, earth endures" — said ever since of anything one hopes will last. The box is "enduring"</span></div>
  </div>
  <figcaption class="citation">
    <em>Laozi</em> 5 and 7.
    <code>urn:nabu:kanripo:KR5c0057:005:1a</code> · license: attribution.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The cold line runs on machinery you own outright:
<span class="script">天地</span> sharing the subject seat,
<span class="script">不仁</span> denying the doing — heaven and
earth do not *do* humaneness — and then
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/102/07-with-by-from-at/' | relative_url }}">chapter 07's</a>
<span class="script">以</span>…<span class="script">為</span>
frame at its most unsentimental: "take the ten thousand things
*as* straw dogs." The kind line is two tiny clauses,
<span class="script">天長</span> and <span class="script">地</span>▢:
a subject and a bare quality-word — "heaven longs, earth
endures" — no "is" anywhere, because in this language a quality
predicates all by itself.

## The wind over the grass

<figure class="reading reading--script">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">君子之▢風。</span><span class="translit pinyin">jūn zǐ zhī dé fēng</span><span class="gloss">"the lord's character is wind" — and the people, the verse continues, are grass: when the wind crosses the field, the grass bends. Rule by example, not by executions; the box is "character/virtue"</span></div>
  </div>
  <figcaption class="citation">
    <em>Analects</em> 12.19 — the sentence after the wanting
    that makes the people good.
    <code>urn:nabu:kanripo:KR1h0004:012:30a</code> · license: attribution.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Look at how little it needs: a thread-phrase subject — "the
noble person's character" — and then one bare noun,
<span class="script">風</span>. No verb, no
<span class="script">也</span>, not even the frame's furniture:
at this compression the nominal sentence is just two things set
side by side, exactly as <span class="script">君君</span> was in
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/102/01-the-sentence-without-a-verb/' | relative_url }}">chapter 01</a>.
Character *is* wind; the metaphor and the grammar are the same
gesture.

## The ladder's crown

The decade ladder this course began climbing in its counting
chapter promised you its last rung when 從 arrived. It has:

<figure class="reading reading--script">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">七十而從心所欲，不▢▢。</span><span class="translit pinyin">qī shí ér cóng xīn suǒ yù, bù yú jǔ</span><span class="gloss">"at seventy, I followed whatever my heart wanted — and never crossed the line" — the end of the story that began at fifteen: a whole life practicing until wanting the right thing became effortless. The boxes are "overstep" and "the carpenter's square = the rules"</span></div>
  </div>
  <figcaption class="citation">
    <em>Analects</em> 2.4, the final clause — the ladder
    complete.
    <code>urn:nabu:kanripo:KR1h0004:002:2a</code> · license: attribution.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The rung's frame you know — number, hinge, deed. The deed:
<span class="script">從</span> "follow," and its object is
built from two old machines pressed tight —
<span class="script">心所欲</span>, "what the heart wants," the
<span class="script">所</span>-phrase with its owner
<span class="script">心</span> set directly against it, no
thread needed at this compression. Then the coda:
<span class="script">不</span> and two boxes, "never oversteps
the carpenter's square." Follow every want and break no rule:
the sentence holds the paradox in one breath, and the grammar
gives the wanting and the rule equal footing — which is
precisely the achievement being described.

## We did it ourselves

The course closes where the best government disappears. Under
the ruler nobody notices, the work is finished — and the people
all say one thing:

<figure class="reading reading--script reading--stacked">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">▢成事▢，而百▢皆謂我自然。</span><span class="translit pinyin">gōng chéng shì suì, ér bǎi xìng jiē wèi wǒ zì rán</span><span class="gloss">"the work is done, the task complete — and the people all say: we did it ourselves" — the highest praise of leadership: to lead so lightly that no one feels led. The boxes are "achievement", "follow through", and "families" — 百-families, "the hundred families," is classical for everyone</span></div>
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">道▢自然。</span><a class="say" href="{{ '/assets/audio/lines/102-15-dao-fa-zi-ran.mp3' | relative_url }}" title="hear it"><span class="translit pinyin">dào fǎ zì rán</span></a><span class="gloss">"the way follows what is so of itself" — the Laozi's last rung: people follow earth, earth follows heaven, heaven follows the way, and the way follows nothing but its own nature. The box is "follows-the-model-of"</span></div>
  </div>
  <figcaption class="citation">
    <em>Laozi</em> 17 and 25.
    <code>urn:nabu:kanripo:KR5c0057:017:1a</code> · license: attribution.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

In the people's boast, walk the last clause:
<span class="script">而百</span>▢<span class="script">皆謂</span> —
"and the hundred families all say" —
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/102/14-all-of-it/' | relative_url }}">chapter 14's</a>
<span class="script">皆</span> spreading the saying over
everyone. What they say is three characters:
<span class="script">我自然</span> — "we, of ourselves, so":
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/102/07-with-by-from-at/' | relative_url }}">chapter 07's</a>
reflexive <span class="script">自</span> joined to this
chapter's <span class="script">然</span>. That little compound,
*self-so*, went on to become the everyday word for nature
itself — the world as the thing that does itself. And the
*Laozi*'s last rung runs the chain your practice line began:
<span class="script">道</span>▢<span class="script">自然</span>,
the way takes *self-so* as its model — the boxed verb "follows
the model of" is the only word in the line you do not own.

## Where this leaves you

Fifteen chapters of grammar, and 138 characters — two of every
five on a classical page. You can define, act, deny, count,
own, name, hitch, finish, ask, infer, want, cause, compare,
total, and conclude — in the language of the *Analects* and the
*Laozi*, on their own sentences. The course keeps going; the
page keeps opening.
