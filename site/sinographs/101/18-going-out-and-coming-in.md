here, out, in, east, and on the way back:

在 plants presence in the earth; 出 and 入 are the two
directions of every door. 方 fans out from "quarter of the
world" — 四方, the four quarters, is the classical way to say
*everywhere*. 東 is where the sun comes up, however the graph
began. And 來 is this chapter's quiet rescue of an old friend:
a wheat plant standing in for "come" since before writing
settled.

## Friends from the far quarters

The second clause of the *Analects*' opening has walked with you
since <a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/10-the-sentences-tools/' | relative_url }}">chapter 10</a>.
With 方 and 來 it comes into its full shape:

## Knowing without going

The *Laozi* takes the door and refuses it — 出 in its plainest
work, 天下 from your first chapters:

## Where this leaves you

A hundred and four characters — very nearly one in three of the
classical corpus. The stage is cast and placed. Next comes the
word for learning itself, the seasons it keeps, and the promise
the first page made you — read whole.
