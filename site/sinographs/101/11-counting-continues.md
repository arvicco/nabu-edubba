# Counting continues

In <a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/00-orientation/' | relative_url }}">chapter 00</a>
you learned 一二三 in one breath — three strokes for three
numbers, the writing system at its most honest. It could not go
on that way: a pile of six strokes is harder to read than a
word. So the numbers past three changed strategy:

四 and 六 are borrowed graphs, like the particles of the last
stretch — counting words wore out their pictures early. 五 is a
knot tied between heaven and earth. And 百 is this course's
first *built* number: white for the sound, one stroke for the
tally. Numbers stack the way you would hope: 五十 fifty, 三百
three hundred.

## A life in decades

You read the master's "at fifteen I set my heart on learning" in
spirit long ago; now the later decades are yours almost box-free
— and all three of today's numbers report for duty:

Read the numbers aloud as they pass: sì shí, wǔ shí, liù shí —
forty, fifty, sixty. The pattern is the whole lesson: number
before 十 multiplies it.

## Where the count stands

Six digits, the ten, and the hundred: every count they compose —
fifty, three hundred, sixty-four — is now yours to write. Seven,
eight, nine, and the thousands wait their turn. Next chapter
turns from counting to *seeing*: the eye, the glance between two
people, the head — and the road the head travels, which the
classics call the Way.
