# Stopping at true

A footprint grows into a philosophy in three steps, and the
evening names itself:

Read the chain aloud: the foot stops — 止. The foot stops at the
line, exactly where it should — 正, upright. The sun stands over
the upright foot — 是, what is squarely so: *this*. Three
characters, each built on the last, ending in one of the
commonest words in the language. And 名 is the chapter's second
compound story: evening 夕 beside mouth 口 — when the light is
gone and your face is not enough, you *say your name*.

## The rectification of names

Asked what he would do first if given a government, the Master
said: get the names right. The sentence that follows is the most
famous statement of *why* in the tradition — and its first two
clauses are nearly yours:

## Knowing where to stop

止 is not just halting — it is knowing one's resting place. The
Great Learning builds its whole staircase on that:

## The saying with no boxes

<a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/06-knowing-and-walking/' | relative_url }}">Chapter 06</a> gave you a fragment and a promise. Here is the whole
saying — eleven characters, and not one of them is a box:

<figure class="reading reading--script reading--stacked">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">知之為知之，不知為不知，是知也。</span><span class="translit pinyin">zhī zhī wéi zhī zhī, bù zhī wéi bù zhī, shì zhī yě</span><span class="gloss">"to know a thing, take it as known; not to know, take as not known — THIS is knowing." Every character taught, every character yours: the longest fully open reading in the course, sealed by today's 是 and closed by 也's bell</span></div>
  </div>
  <figcaption class="citation">
    <em>Analects</em> 2.17 — the promise of
    <a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/06-knowing-and-walking/' | relative_url }}">chapter 06</a>,

    <code>urn:nabu:kanripo:KR1h0004:002:18a</code> · license: attribution.

Stop on that for a moment — the way 止 stops. Eight chapters ago
you could not read a stroke. This is a whole saying of Confucius,
word for word, in the original, and the only help you needed was
the order in which you learned things.

## Where this goes
