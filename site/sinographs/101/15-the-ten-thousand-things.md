# The ten thousand things

<a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/01-the-order-of-strokes/' | relative_url }}">chapter 01</a>;
then an ox walks in carrying the word for *things* — and the
scorpion at the end of the table counts them all:

七, 八 and 九 are all loans — small pictures whose first
meanings (a cut, a parting, a crooked elbow) were spent long ago
on the numbers. With them the set
一二三四五六七八九十 stands complete. 物 is this course's
second sound-plus-sense character after
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/04-roots-tips-borrowed-sounds/' | relative_url }}">chapter 04's</a>
味: the ox 牛 says what kind of thing it is, the banner 勿 says
how it sounds. And 萬 — ten thousand — is how the classics say
*countless*: 萬物, "the ten thousand things," is their name for
everything alive under heaven.

## The oxen come home

牛's oldest song is an evening scene — a wife counting the herd
down the hill while her husband serves far away, with 夕 from
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/08-stopping-at-true/' | relative_url }}">chapter 08</a>
setting the hour:

## Three begets the ten thousand

In <a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/12-the-eye-and-the-way/' | relative_url }}">chapter 12</a>
the *Laozi*'s begetting chain ended in two boxes. They were 萬物
— and now the chain runs to its end with nothing hidden:

<figure class="reading reading--script">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">道生一，一生二，二生三，三生萬物。</span><span class="translit pinyin">dào shēng yī, yī shēng èr, èr shēng sān, sān shēng wàn wù</span><span class="gloss">"the Way begets one, one begets two, two begets three, three begets the ten thousand things" — every character yours, for the first time</span></div>
  </div>

And a second whole *Laozi* saying follows at once, built from the
same pieces — 有 and 無, being and not-being, from
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/07-doing-and-being/' | relative_url }}">chapter 07</a>:

<figure class="reading reading--script">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">天下萬物生於有，有生於無。</span><span class="translit pinyin">tiān xià wàn wù shēng yú yǒu, yǒu shēng yú wú</span><span class="gloss">"the ten thousand things under heaven are born from being — and being is born from non-being" — trace anything back far enough, says the Laozi, and you reach not a first thing but the open emptiness things come FROM: the claim that made 無 a cornerstone of Chinese thought. Eleven characters, all yours</span></div>

## Where this leaves you

Eighty-seven characters, and the counting words are done: any
number the classics care to write, you can read. Next the course
turns to what the tradition did with its words — the old, the
cause, the pattern, and the two books it never stopped quoting.
