# Sinographs 101 · Foundations

**The first course of the sinograph school.** No prerequisites. By
the end you will know how the characters work as a writing system —
strokes, components, and the ways a character can mean — own a first
inventory of real characters, and read genuine lines of the
classical written language they were made for.

This school takes the classical road: the characters are taught on
the texts they wrote for their first two thousand years, not on
modern newspaper vocabulary. If what you want is modern Chinese or
Japanese, excellent tools already exist and the Reference will point
you to them; what this course gives you is the older, longer story
those tools skip — and everything you learn here counts toward the
modern languages too, because the characters are the same.

**The course is complete.** Ten chapters and a reference: how the
characters work, from the first stroke to the master's opening
sentence — 55 characters, one of every five on a classical page,
and every way a character can mean. The reading itself continues
in [102 · Literary Chinese]({{ '/sinographs/102/' | relative_url }}),
where the grammar of the classical language takes over.
