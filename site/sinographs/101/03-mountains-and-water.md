# Mountains and water

The world the classical books look out on, in three characters:

    </tr>

山 and 水 are pictographs of the first order — you can still see
the peaks and the current. 中 is a pointing character like your
numbers: nothing looks like *middle*, so the character plants a
pole through it. Together they make the script's oldest kind of
poetry possible: 山水, "mountains-and-water," is to this day the
Chinese word for landscape — the thing a landscape painting is a
painting *of* — and 山中, "amid the mountains," is where the poems
and the hermits live. Two characters each, and you read both
whole.

## The wise and the humane

A saying built on your new pair — from the *Analects*, the sayings
of Confucius, the book this course will return to more than any
other. The speaker formula that opens it is still two chapters
away, so the reading begins inside the quotation marks:

<figure class="reading reading--script">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">▢▢▢水，▢▢▢山。</span><span class="translit pinyin">zhī zhě yào shuǐ, rén zhě yào shān</span><span class="gloss">"the wise delight in water; the humane delight in mountains" — perfectly parallel clauses, your two landscapes holding down the rhyme-seats</span></div>
  </div>
  <figcaption class="citation">
    <em>Analects</em> 6.23.
    <code>urn:nabu:kanripo:KR1h0004:006:30a</code> · license: attribution.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Note what the parallelism does: once you know 水 ends the first
clause and 山 the second, you know the two boxed words before each
are doing the same jobs — *who* delights, and *delights*. Classical
prose leans on this symmetry constantly; the boxes are already
teaching you grammar.

## The sun in the middle

And 中 in its oldest work, from the Changes — noon as the middle
of the sky:

<figure class="reading reading--script">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">日中▢▢。</span><span class="translit pinyin">rì zhōng zé zè</span><span class="gloss">"the sun at noon begins to slant" — 日中, "sun-middle," is noon; what is full, begins to lean. Four characters, half of them yours</span></div>
  </div>
  <figcaption class="citation">
    <em>Zhouyi</em>, hexagram Feng, in the compilation <em>Zhouyi jijie</em>.
    <code>urn:nabu:kanripo:KR1a0008:011:14a</code> · license: attribution.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

中 will not stay in the landscape: 中人 "an average person,"
中天 "mid-heaven," and eventually the name the civilization gave
itself — the middle kingdom — all hang on this one planted stroke.
