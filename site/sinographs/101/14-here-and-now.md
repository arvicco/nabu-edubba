down — to a place, and to the present:

此 points at the near thing. 所 is 者's mirror from
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/09-the-masters-opening/' | relative_url }}">chapter 09</a>:
者 turns a verb into its doer, 所 turns it into its
*where* or its *what* — 所知, "what is known". 於 is 于 grown
up: the same little preposition the later classics dress more
fully. 至 is the arrow landing; 至於 together means "coming down
to" — and 今 is where every sentence is spoken from.

<a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/10-the-sentences-tools/' | relative_url }}">chapter 10</a>

## Where this leaves you

Eighty-one characters — one in every four characters of the
classical corpus. These chapters began with the sentence's
biggest tool and end with its finest: the words that say *here*,
*where*, *reaching*, *now*. Ahead lies the family that talks:
the words that grow on 言, and with them longer runs of the
texts you have been reading in pieces.
