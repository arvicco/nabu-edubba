# The work and its doing

You have had the doing-word 為 since the sentence chapters. This
chapter hires its whole crew — the task, the making, the
finishing, the being-able, and the sending of someone to do it:

事 is the job itself and, turned toward a person, the serving of
them. 作 makes and originates; 成 finishes and becomes; 能 can.
And 使 hands the job to somebody — send him, employ her, cause
it. The bear in 能 is the sign lists' honest drawing: the animal
lent its body to "ability" so early that it later had to grow a
new character of its own to stay a bear.

## Doing without doing

The *Laozi*'s most famous advice arrives, and you can read every
character of it — no boxes at all:

## Hard and easy finish each other

The *Laozi*'s list of pairs that make each other — begun with
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/20-first-and-after-then/' | relative_url }}">chapter 20's</a>
front-and-back — runs through this chapter's words too:

<figure class="reading reading--script reading--stacked">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">故有無相生，▢▢相成。</span><span class="translit pinyin">gù yǒu wú xiāng shēng, nán yì xiāng chéng</span><span class="gloss">"so having and lacking give birth to each other; hard and easy complete each other" — you only know a task is hard because an easy one sits beside it. ## Able to be great — by not trying to be

能 and 成 share one famous sentence about the way water-like
greatness works:

<figure class="reading reading--script reading--stacked">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">是以▢人之能成大也，</span><span class="translit pinyin">shì yǐ shèng rén zhī néng chéng dà yě,</span><span class="gloss">"this is how the wise person is able to become great:"</span></div>

## Where this leaves you

A hundred and twenty-one characters, and the crew of doing is
hired. Next: wanting and getting — and the *Laozi*'s people, who
finish the work and say they did it all themselves.
