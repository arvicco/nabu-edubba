# The eye and the Way

Five characters that build on each other like a sentence: an
eye, the eye seeing, the eye measuring another, a head — and
the head on the road:

目 alone is the organ; give it legs and it acts: 見, to see. Set
it before a tree and it appraises: 相, which the classics
sharpened into *each other* — seeing that runs both ways. 首 is
the head that does the seeing; and 道 is the head under way — a
road, and then *the* road: the Way things go.

## The Way counts to three

The *Laozi*'s cosmology runs through your numbers — one, two,
three from
<a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/00-orientation/' | relative_url }}">chapter 00</a>,
生 from the last stretch, and now the word that starts it all:

<figure class="reading reading--script">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">道生一，一生二，二生三，三生▢▢。</span><span class="translit pinyin">dào shēng yī, yī shēng èr, èr shēng sān, sān shēng wàn wù</span><span class="gloss">"the Way begets one, one begets two, two begets three, three begets the ten thousand things" — two boxes for the "ten thousand things", the classical phrase for everything there is</span></div>

    <em>Laozi</em> 42.

Twelve characters, ten of them yours. Read it aloud once for the
grammar and once for the rhythm — the chain 生…生…生 is the
oldest begat-list in Chinese.

## Each other

相's first job in the canon's most famous chain of opposites —
where having and not-having, from the last stretch, give birth
to each other:

<figure class="reading reading--script">
  <div class="reading-lines">

## The head you cannot see

見 and 首 in one breath — the *Laozi* on the Way itself, which
has no face to meet and no back to follow:

## Seeing and not doing

about ghosts and courage whose first half needs one more

## Where the road goes

The Way has arrived, and with it this course's deepest word.
You have now read the *Laozi* describing the Way's begettings,
its facelessness, and its taste for opposites — all in the
original. Next chapter turns to liking and likening: the woman,
the beloved compound she anchors, and how the classics say
*as* and *is-not*.
