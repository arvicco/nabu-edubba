女 kneels; beside a child she is 好, the script's picture of
*good* — which the classics mostly read as the verb hào, *to
love*. Beside a mouth she lends her old sound and becomes 如,
*to be like*. 云 opens quotations, 非 denies whole statements
(不 says "does not"; 非 says "is not"), and 矣 closes a sentence
the way 也 does — but where 也 states, 矣 announces that
something has *become* so.

## Done, says the arrow

矣 needs no new sentence — it has been waiting inside one you
already read. <a href="{{ '/sinographs/101/10-the-sentences-tools/' | relative_url }}">Chapter 10</a>'s
teacher-line ended with two boxes; the second
was 矣, and now it has a name: 可以為▢矣 — "you can, by this, be
a teacher — *and it is so*." Where 也 lays a fact on the table,
矣 tells you the arrow has landed: the state has arrived, the
count is complete, the decade has turned.

## Where this leaves the sentence

Likeness, love, denial, quotation, arrival: the sentence can now
compare, refuse, and quote its elders. Next come
the words of *here* and *now*, and with them a
place-maker that turns verbs into places the way 者 turns them
into people.
