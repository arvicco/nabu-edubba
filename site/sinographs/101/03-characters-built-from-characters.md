# Characters built from characters

Eighteen simple characters were the price of admission. Here is
what they buy — five characters you can take apart on sight:

Read the parts aloud as you meet each one: 明 is 日 and 月; 旦 is
日 over 一; 休 is 人 against 木; 仁 is 人 beside 二; 品 is 口
three times. **Nothing in this table is new** — only the
combinations are. This is the third of the script's four moves:
put two meanings together and let them make a third. How do you
draw *bright*? You don't; you set the sun and the moon side by
side.

you read the wise and the humane through boxes. One of those boxes
was 仁 — the word for the deepest virtue in the Confucian world,

Notice 仁 and 人 say the same syllable, *rén* — the classics lean
on that pun on purpose ("humaneness is being-a-person"). Hold that
sound-echo: it is a clue to the fourth move, and the fourth move
is the whole rest of the script.

## Where this goes

Twenty-three characters. The last move remains — the one that
built nine of every ten characters in the dictionary: borrowing a
character for nothing but its **sound**. Next chapter, your tree
grows a root and a tip, and then it starts to sing.
