# Orientation: the road to reading

Welcome back to the Egyptian school. You arrive with
[101's]({{ '/hieroglyphs/101/' | relative_url }}) fifty-three signs,
its conventions, and its habits — and this course assumes all of
them without apology. 102 is a different kind of course: a
**literacy track**, the same design as the cuneiform school's
[102]({{ '/cuneiform/102/' | relative_url }}). Fewer grand tours,
more mileage. Each chapter grows your inventory by a computed
batch, adds exactly as much Middle Egyptian grammar as its readings
demand, and puts real lines in front of you — from tales, from
doctors' prescriptions, from temple walls.

## The marks, refreshed in one breath

101's [orientation primer]({{ '/hieroglyphs/101/00-orientation/' | relative_url }})
already taught every mark this track uses: the Leiden letters
(*ꜣ ꜥ ḥ ḫ ẖ š ṯ ḏ*), the no-vowels rule and the "insert an e" habit
for saying words aloud, the **≡** that joins a suffix to its word
(*zꜣ≡f*, "his son" — the corpora print it `=`), the editors' dot
(*msi̯*) and comma (*zꜣ,t*). Flip back there whenever a mark looks
unfamiliar; nothing new is sprung on you here. One promise for this
track: when a verb starts carrying those suffixes — and in Middle
Egyptian they carry the whole story —
[chapter 02]({{ '/hieroglyphs/102/02-his-majesty-says/' | relative_url }})
slows down and takes the machinery apart properly.

## Your first readings of the track

From the Late Period's historical inscriptions, a god's own boast —
and your new sky-sign closing its own word:

Piece by piece:

- **jnk** — "I": the independent pronoun, here in a compressed
  spelling the corpus records — the pot 𓏌 and basket 𓎡 carrying
  its *n* and *k*. Egyptian says "I am X" by putting *jnk* first
  and X after it, nothing else needed — a whole sentence type the
  very next chapter opens properly.
- **nb** — "lord": 101's basket 𓎟, exactly as in every "lord of"
  title you read there.
- **p,t** — "the sky": *p* 𓊪 and *t* 𓏏 spell the sound, and then
  your new 𓇯 stands at the end — the sky-slab closing the word
  *sky*, sound-signs below, the thing itself above, the whole
  system of 101 in one word.

And from the other end of the cosmos — the court of Akhenaten at
Amarna, where this is what one did before the king:

  </div>

</figure>

- **sn** — "to kiss": spelled *s* 𓊃, *n* 𓈖, with a *t* 𓏏
  trailing on this form; kissing the ground is what an official's
  day at court began with.
- **tꜣ** — "the earth": your new land-sign, alone, carrying the
  whole word.
- **n** — "for": the little preposition, 101's water-ripple.
- **nb-tꜣ,wj** — "the Lord of the Two Lands": the basket again,
  and 𓇿 again — the royal title that names Upper and Lower Egypt
  in one breath. One line, and your land-sign has already worked
  twice.

Lord of the sky; lord of the two lands. Two new signs, and the
whole vertical axis of the Egyptian world — what the gods hold and
what the king holds — is yours to read.

## How this course works

- **Batches are computed, not curated by whim** — and
  [101's Reference]({{ '/hieroglyphs/101/12-reference/' | relative_url }})
  still holds every sign's data.
- **Grammar arrives in bites**, only when a reading needs it. The
  full grammar lives in the standard references (this track leans
  on James P. Allen's *Middle Egyptian*, cited once here for the
  whole course).[^allen]
- **Readings are real**, cited, license-checked, and rendered in
  full hieroglyphs the moment the inventory allows — the ▢ boxes
  of 101 are already rare here, because 101 did its job.
