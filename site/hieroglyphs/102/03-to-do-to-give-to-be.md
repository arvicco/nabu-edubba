# To do, to give, to be

[Chapter 02]({{ '/hieroglyphs/102/02-his-majesty-says/' | relative_url }})
built the verb machine and ran it on one verb, "say." This chapter
hands you the three stems that do more work than any others in the
language — and by its end, one god's name will be exactly half
yours.

## Grammar bite — three stems, one machine

No new machinery today: these stems ride the suffix conjugation
exactly as *ḏd* did. *jri̯≡f* "he does," *ḏi̯≡s* "she gives,"
*wnn≡k* "you exist." Two small habits are worth naming before the
readings use them:

- **The order form.** Drop the suffix entirely and aim the bare
  stem at someone — *jri̯!* — and you have Egyptian's imperative:
  "do!" No politeness particles required; Egypt ran on these.
- **r + verb — "in order to."** The preposition *r* in front of a
  verb's noun-form turns it into a purpose: *r ḏi̯.t* — "to
  give, in order to give." Hold the pattern loosely; the grammars
  file its parts under the infinitive, and this course needs only
  the sense.

## As the sky exists

Back to Amarna, and the hymn this course read in
[chapter 01]({{ '/hieroglyphs/102/01-it-is-water/' | relative_url }})
— the one where the king is born like the Aten. A few lines on, it
makes the pharaoh a promise built entirely from your signs:

- **wnn** — "exists": your new hare 𓃹 with its *n* complement 𓈖
  — the stem doubled to *wnn*, the shape this verb takes for
  lasting truths.
- **p,t** — "the sky":
  [chapter 00's]({{ '/hieroglyphs/102/00-orientation/' | relative_url }})
  word, letter for letter — *p*, *t*, and the sky-slab riding its
  own word. No suffix on this *wnn*: the sky itself is the
  subject, named in full.
- **wnn≡k** — "you will exist": the same stem, now wearing
  chapter 02's machinery — *≡k*, "you," the king addressed.

Sky-exists, you-exist: two verbal sentences laid side by side, and
the parallelism *is* the promise. Egyptian eternity is grammar.

## Rising, in order to give

The same corpus, the sun himself as subject — and the giving arm
at work inside a purpose:

- **wbn≡k** — "you rise": *w-b-n* spelled out, the sun-disk 𓇳
  classifying the rising, and *≡k* — the Aten himself is "you"
  here.
- **r ḏi̯.t** — "in order to give": the bite's purpose pattern,
  live — *r*, then your new arm 𓂞 with a *t* on its noun-form.
- **n≡f** — "to him": the little *n* as "to, for" before a
  suffix — the king receives.
- **nḥḥ** — "eternity": compressed on this stone to the twisted
  wick 𓎛 and the sun 𓇳 — the corpus's own spelling, thinner
  than the word, and honestly noted.

## Do your work!

Down from the sky entirely. On the walls of Old Kingdom tombs,
overseers stand behind their work gangs, and the captions record
what overseers have said in every century since:

    · license: attribution.

- **jri̯** — "do!": your new eye, bare — the order form from the
  bite, no suffix, aimed straight at somebody.
- **š≡k** — "your work": the pool-sign 𓈙 you have had since 101,
  with "your" attached.
- **rf** — "then, indeed": a little pushing particle — the edge
  in the foreman's voice, written down forty-five centuries ago.

One wall, one shout. The same eye-verb that gives eternity in a
royal hymn tells a field hand to hurry up. That range — sky to
mud, in one stem — is what "hardest-working verb" means.

## Half a god

Now the promised cliff. Egypt's great god of the dead is Osiris —
*Wsjr* — and his name is regularly written with exactly two
signs: the eye you learned today, and a throne. The eye is yours;
the throne is
[chapter 04's]({{ '/hieroglyphs/102/04-saying-no/' | relative_url }}).
You are one sign away from reading the name that ruled the
Egyptian afterlife — and the chapter that teaches it is, fittingly,
the one about death, negation, and hearts weighed. Bring your eye.

## Drill before you go on

1. Draw 𓁹, 𓂞, 𓃹 from memory; say *jr*, *dj*, *wn*.
2. Conjugate *ḏi̯* through *≡j*, *≡k*, *≡f*, *≡sn* aloud — then
   put chapter 02's *.jn* into one of them and translate it.
3. Read *wnn p,t wnn≡k* cold, covering the gloss, and explain why
   the first *wnn* wears no suffix.
4. Compose in transliteration, on the bite's pattern: "you rise in
   order to do it" — inventing nothing but the pronoun.
