## Where it comes from

A lasso — the throwing rope open in its loop. It writes *wꜣ*,
the sound of distance and roads (*wꜣ,t*, "way"); and in the
Ptolemaic rings it was drafted to carry the O of the royal
Greek names.

## How to remember it

The **lasso** flies far before it lands — *wꜣ* is the far-away
sound, the first half of "way." A rope thrown toward the
horizon: distance, drawn.

Piece by piece:

- **Jmi̯,t** — the name's first element, opened by the giving
  arm
- **-wꜣj** — the lasso with its vulture and reed, closing it

A woman's name with the rope-of-distance inside it, four and a
half thousand years old. The sign is taught in
<a href="{{ '/hieroglyphs/101/11-the-stone-that-talked/' | relative_url }}">101's chapter 11</a>.
