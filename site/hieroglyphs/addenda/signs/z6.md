## Where it comes from

A plain diagonal stroke, borrowed from the fast hieratic hand —
and used as a substitute: where a proper death-sign or
enemy-sign ought to be drawn, scribes often refused. A dangerous
picture might act. So they pressed this harmless mark instead
and kept the inventory honest.

## How to remember it

**Death's stand-in** is the script flinching: the scribes would
not draw the corpse, the enemy, the doomed god — so a neutral
stroke stands where the danger was. When you meet it, read what
was avoided.

Piece by piece:

- **nn** — shall not be
- **rn.pl≡tn** — your names: enemy names, so the classifier that
  would normally draw their fate is this cautious stroke instead

Even cursing the enemies, the scribe would not draw their
picture. The habit is taught in
<a href="{{ '/hieroglyphs/102/04-saying-no/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 04</a>.
