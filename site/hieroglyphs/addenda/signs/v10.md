## Where it comes from

A loop of rope with its ends tied off — stretched into an oval
frame around a king's name. It reads nothing; it *encloses*: the
rope that rings the name marks everything inside as royal, a
circuit of protection the texts themselves call *šnw*, "that
which the sun encircles."

## How to remember it

The **cartouche** is a rope fence around the most valuable words
in Egypt. Wherever you see the ring, a king or queen lives
inside it — the frame is the crown.

## In the wild

The cartouche never stands alone: in the corpus it appears only
wrapped around royal names, and every such name carries signs
beyond this course's inventory — so this one page shows no
attested line, rather than show one broken by boxes. Its working
life is on display in
<a href="{{ '/hieroglyphs/101/05-names-in-rings/' | relative_url }}">101's chapter 05</a>,
where Ptolemy's ring cracked the decipherment, and on the
<a href="{{ '/hieroglyphs/101/11-the-stone-that-talked/' | relative_url }}">Rosetta Stone</a>
itself.
