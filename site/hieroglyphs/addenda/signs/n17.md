## Where it comes from

A flat strip of earth — the alluvial plain in cross-section,
sometimes dotted with grains of soil. It writes *tꜣ*, "land,
earth": the Two Lands (*tꜣ,wj*) are Egypt itself, and to "kiss
the earth" before the king is to press your face to this strip.

## How to remember it

The **land** is a floor as the
[sky]({{ '/hieroglyphs/addenda/signs/n1/' | relative_url }}) is
a ceiling — the world's other slab. Everything Egyptian happens
between the two flat lines.

Piece by piece:

- **sn tꜣ** — kissing the earth: this strip, being kissed
- **n nb-tꜣ,wj** — for the Lord of the Two Lands: the same
  strip, compressed inside the royal title

The land twice: once under the courtiers' faces, once in the
king's own title. The line is read in
<a href="{{ '/hieroglyphs/102/00-orientation/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 00</a>.
