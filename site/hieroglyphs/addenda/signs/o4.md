## Where it comes from

A reed shelter seen in plan — a light field hut, one open side.
It writes *h*, the ordinary breathy h of "hat": the easiest
sound in the Egyptian column of h-sounds, matched to the
lightest of its buildings.

## How to remember it

A **shelter** of reeds barely holds back the wind — and *h* is
barely a sound, just breath through an open door. Light
building, light letter.

Piece by piece:

- **tw≡tw** — one, people in general
- **hꜣy.ṱ** — goes down: the shelter-sign opening the verb, the
  walking legs closing it
- **r mw** — to the water

Everyday motion — down the bank to the river — with the hut-sign
breathing the verb open. The sign is taught in
<a href="{{ '/hieroglyphs/101/04-your-first-signs/' | relative_url }}">101's chapter 04</a>.
