## Where it comes from

A tethering rope — a cord with looped ends for hobbling an
animal. It writes *ṯ*, the "tj" sound of British "tune": a *t*
with a little pull in it, matched to a rope that pulls.

## How to remember it

The **tether** is t with a twist of rope on it — *ṯ*. Where the
loaf's plain *t* sits still, the tether-sound tugs; it holds
the enclitic "you" (*ṯw*) the way a rope holds a calf.

Piece by piece:

- **wni̯** — hurry: the hare, running as ever
- **ṯw** — you: the tether-sign catching the person being
  hurried

A shouted order on a tomb wall — and the rope-sound lassoes the
"you" it is aimed at. The sign is taught in
<a href="{{ '/hieroglyphs/101/04-your-first-signs/' | relative_url }}">101's chapter 04</a>.
