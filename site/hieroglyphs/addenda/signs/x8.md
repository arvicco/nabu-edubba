## Where it comes from

A tall conical loaf — bread shaped for presenting, not eating.
It writes *ḏi̯*, the short form of "to give": the offered loaf
standing for the act of offering itself. Its cousin
[D37]({{ '/hieroglyphs/addenda/signs/d37/' | relative_url }})
draws the arm doing the handing; this sign keeps only the gift.

## How to remember it

The **given loaf** is the present with the giver cropped out —
just the bread, mid-air, changing hands. Wherever gods or kings
give life, bread, or breath, this little cone does the giving.

Piece by piece:

- **ḥtp** — the offering, loaf on its mat
- **ḏi̯** — gives: this loaf, doing the verb
- **nswt** — the king

Two loaves in a row — one resting, one given — open the formula
that fed the dead. It is taught in
<a href="{{ '/hieroglyphs/101/06-the-offering-formula/' | relative_url }}">101's chapter 06</a>.
