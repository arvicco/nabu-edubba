## Where it comes from

A lion lying down, forepaws stretched — resting but watching. It
writes *rw*; and because Egyptian had no separate *l*, the lion
was drafted for the L of foreign names — the L in Ptolemy and
Cleopatra's rings is this animal.

## How to remember it

The **lion** at rest still owns the ground — and it owns two
alphabets' worth of sounds: Egyptian *rw* at home, and the
borrowed *L* whenever Greeks came visiting. Decipherment walked
in on its back.

Piece by piece:

- **ꜥꜣpp ḫr** — Apophis is fallen, every dangerous name written
  with the cautious substitute stroke
- **qrnr** — one of the serpent's ugly by-names, the lion
  carrying its inner r

A liturgy fells the chaos-snake, and the lion-sign helps spell
the monster's name. The sign is taught in
<a href="{{ '/hieroglyphs/101/11-the-stone-that-talked/' | relative_url }}">101's chapter 11</a>.
