## Where it comes from

A single ripple of water — the zigzag line every child draws for
a wave. It writes *n*: the genitive "of," the dative "to, for,"
and the *n* inside half the words in the language. Stack three
ripples and you get
[N35A]({{ '/hieroglyphs/addenda/signs/n35a/' | relative_url }}),
water itself.

## How to remember it

One **ripple** joins two banks — and *n* joins two words: "the
spell OF water," "give it TO him." Wherever Egyptian needs a
little bridge, a line of water runs under it.

Piece by piece:

- **rʾ** — spell, utterance — literally "mouth"
- **n** — of: this ripple, doing the genitive
- **mw** — water

The ripple bridging a spell to its subject — which happens to be
water. The indirect genitive is taught in
<a href="{{ '/hieroglyphs/102/01-it-is-water/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 01</a>.
