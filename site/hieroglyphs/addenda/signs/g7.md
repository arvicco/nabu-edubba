## Where it comes from

A falcon perched on the carrying-standard — Horus on his pole,
as carried in procession. Silent: it classifies gods, kings, and
divine names, and in later texts even the pronouns that refer to
them. Wherever it perches, divinity is present.

## How to remember it

The **watcher** sits above the word the way the falcon sat above
Egypt — not read, just present. If the falcon oversees a name,
someone divine is being spoken of; mind your manners in the
sentence.

Piece by piece:

- **jw≡j rḫ.kw** — I know, and have known
- **rn≡t** — your name: a divine name, so the falcon perches on
  the word itself

To know a god's name was power — and the watcher marks whose
name is being claimed. The sign is taught in
<a href="{{ '/hieroglyphs/102/02-his-majesty-says/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 02</a>.
