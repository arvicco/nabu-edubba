## Where it comes from

A flowering reed — one stem, one bent head of bloom, the marsh
plant of the Nile's edges. It writes *j*, the light glide the
convention reads as "i"; doubled, it strengthens into *y*. One of
the first signs any student of Egyptian ever learns.

## How to remember it

A single **reed** bows its flowered head — as slight as the sound
it writes. Where the wind barely moves through the marsh, the
voice barely moves through *j*.

Piece by piece:

- **mn** — the game-board sign with its
  complement *n*
- **j** — this reed, closing the name

A name Egyptian history knows well — tradition remembers a Meni
(Menes) as the first king of all, though this tomb's Meni was a
private man. The reed is taught in
