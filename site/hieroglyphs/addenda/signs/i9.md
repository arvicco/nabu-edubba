## Where it comes from

The horned viper — *Cerastes*, the desert snake with two little
horns over its eyes, drawn resting. It writes *f*, which makes
it the suffix pronoun *≡f*, "he, his, him": no sign in the
script is attached to more kings.

## How to remember it

The **viper** hisses *f* — a snake sound if ever there was one —
and then coils at the end of words as "his": his house, his
majesty, his life. Small, horned, and possessive.

Piece by piece:

- **ḏd** — says: cobra and hand
- **≡f** — he: this viper, doing its pronoun duty

The commonest two words in Egyptian narration — someone speaks,
and the viper says who. The suffix pronouns are taught in
