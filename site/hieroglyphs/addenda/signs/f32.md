## Where it comes from

An animal's belly with teats and tail — the soft underside of a
beast. It writes *ẖ*, the softer front-of-mouth cousin of
[ḫ]({{ '/hieroglyphs/addenda/signs/aa1/' | relative_url }}), as
in German "ich"; and as a word, *ẖ,t* is "belly, body" — the
picture kept its meaning.

## How to remember it

The **belly** is the soft side — and *ẖ* is the soft rasp,
underneath its hard cousin the way a belly is under a back. Soft
picture, soft sound; and "son of his body" wears it.

Piece by piece:

- **zꜣ≡f** — his son, the duck and the viper
- **n ẖ,t≡f** — of his body: this belly-sign, meaning exactly
  itself
- **Rꜥw-wr** — the son's name, Ra-wer

The formula for a true-born heir: son of his father's own body.
