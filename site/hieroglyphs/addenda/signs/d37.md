## Where it comes from

A forearm with a conical loaf balanced on its open hand — the
act of giving, drawn mid-gesture. It writes *dj/rdj*, "to give,"
sharing the work with the bare
[given loaf]({{ '/hieroglyphs/addenda/signs/x8/' | relative_url }}):
one sign keeps the giver, the other keeps only the gift.

## How to remember it

The **giving arm** reaches out with the bread still on it — the
moment before the gift lands. Life, health, bread, breath:
whatever gods give, this arm is extending it.

Piece by piece:

- **ḏi̯≡f** — may he give: this arm, and the god behind it
- **ꜥnḫ-(w)ḏꜣ-snb** — life, prosperity, health: the blessing
  compressed to three signs

The commonest wish in Egyptian, handed over by the arm built for
it. The formula is read in
<a href="{{ '/hieroglyphs/102/06-life-prosperity-health/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 06</a>;
the sign is taught in
<a href="{{ '/hieroglyphs/102/03-to-do-to-give-to-be/' | relative_url }}">chapter 03</a>.
