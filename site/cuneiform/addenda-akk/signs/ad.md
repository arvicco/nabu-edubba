## Where it comes from

The father-sign — the classic identification, as solid as they
come: Sumerian *ad*, and behind it Akkadian's own *abum*,
"father," a word the two languages all but shared. On syllable
duty Akkadian reads it *ad* or *at* (voiced or voiceless, the
spelling forgives), and it earns steady work closing words like
*ši-ma-at*, "destinies of," and sitting inside the laws'
ever-repeating *i-na-ad-di-in*, "he shall pay."

## How to remember it

The **father** stands at the head of the household and the end of
the ledger: when a payment falls due in the laws — *inaddin*, "he
shall pay" — the father-sign is right there in the middle of the
word, holding the family accountable. The pressed third of the
family, *aṭ*, arrives with the epilogue's
<span class="norm">šaṭram</span>, "the inscribed"
(<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/103/18-the-whole-course/' | relative_url }}">chapter 18</a>) —
ad, at, aṭ: one sign, the whole dental row.
