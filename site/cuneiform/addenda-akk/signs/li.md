## Where it comes from

The old lists gloss the sign as a juniper — an aromatic tree —
though the identification is stated rather than certain. Sumerian
used it sparingly; Akkadian adopted its sound wholesale, and *li*
became the tenth most frequent syllable in Old Babylonian writing:
inside verbs, inside names, and famously inside the wish-forms
(*li-* opens Akkadian's "may he…" precatives, which the next
stretch will read).

## How to remember it

A **juniper** is a modest tree with berries in everything —
gin, stews, incense. The sign is the same: modest shape, spread
through the whole corpus one *li* at a time. Its e-colored
twin *le* rides the same bracket, and first walks in
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/103/08-more-than-one/' | relative_url }}">chapter 08</a>:
<span class="norm">lē'ûm</span>, "the capable," the epilogue's
word for its able king.
