## Where it comes from

The lists connect the shape with cordage — a rope or tether — but
grade the identification cautiously, and its Akkadian life owes
nothing to the picture: it is the sound *um* that made this sign
the ninth most frequent in Old Babylonian writing. The nominative
of nearly every noun ends in -um (*šarrum*, *awīlum*, *ṣīrum*),
and this is the sign that writes it.

## How to remember it

A **tether** holds an animal to its post; -um tethers a noun to
its role as subject. Spot this sign at a word's end and you know
who the sentence is about — the noun is tied down, case settled.
