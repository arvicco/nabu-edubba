## Where it comes from

Not a picture at all but a **ligature**: 𒄿 and 𒀀 welded into
one sign, spelling the glide *ia/ya* that neither could carry
alone. Its great customer is the suffix -ya, "my" — the
first-person voice of every letter-writer in Babylon.

## How to remember it

Two old friends — i and a — standing so close they became one
sign, meaning **my**: what is mine stands that close to me.
Write it whenever the writer claims something for himself.
