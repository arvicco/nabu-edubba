## Where it comes from

The arm-sign of <a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/10-in-those-days/#sign-12009' | relative_url }}">102</a> — *a₂*, "arm, strength, side." Among the
readings Akkadian inherited with it is plain *id*, and Old
Babylonian puts that voice to grim, steady work: *id-di-ma*, "he
laid (the charge), and —"; *id-da-ak*, "he shall be killed." The
strong arm of the law, literally.

## How to remember it

The **arm** does what law 1's verbs describe: it lays the charge
and it carries out the sentence. When a law's verb opens on the
arm, someone is being seized by it. From
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/103/12-say-to-him-a-letter/' | relative_url }}">chapter 12</a>
the arm covers its whole dental family in both vowel colors —
*it*, *ed*, *et*: the perfect's <span class="norm">ittadi</span>,
a name's <span class="norm">ašarēd</span>, the cheek-word
<span class="norm">lēt</span> — one sign, four dress sizes of
one sound.
