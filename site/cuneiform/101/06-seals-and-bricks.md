# Reading real cuneiform: seals and bricks

This is the chapter the course has been climbing toward. The texts
below are not composed for learners: they are transliterations of
actual objects — clay pressed and rolled four thousand years ago —
served from the [Nabu](https://arvicco.github.io/nabu/) library with
their museum identities attached. You will read them the way the
field does — from the transliteration — but never *only* that way:
in Edubba, the cuneiform itself always stands beside the
transliteration. Signs you have not yet been taught appear as ▢, so
you can see exactly how much of the original is already yours.

Write both until they flow; <span class="script">𒈗</span> especially
rewards slow, ordered strokes — big wedges first.

## How a seal works

A cylinder seal is writing you can *roll*: a small stone barrel
carved in mirror image, rolled across damp clay to leave its text
and imagery in relief, as many times as the surface allows. An
official's seal was his signature and his badge of office at once.

Ur III officials rolled such seals onto tablets, tags, and sealings
by the hundred thousand; a seal's text almost always opens with the
king's full titulary and closes with the owner presenting himself as
the king's servant. Learn this one shape and you can read the seals
of an entire dynasty.

The seal you may have already met on this site's specimen page — now
yours to read properly, script beside transliteration. A governor of
Umma, under king Amar-Suen:

Count what is already yours: 𒀭 four times (twice silent before
divine names, once as plain *an*), 𒈗 twice, 𒆠 twice. The
structural skeleton of a royal seal, read at sight.

### Reading 2 — the same formula, wounded

A different tablet from Umma, a different king — and a first
encounter with damage. Square brackets mark signs broken away and
restored by editors; a hash marks a damaged but legible sign:

The owner's name is lost with the top of the sealing, yet the record
still tells us the reign: the tablet it sealed is dated under
Šū-Suen. Notice what you just did — you read a *damaged* ancient text
and extracted history from a formula. That is the working experience
of Assyriology, in miniature.

### Reading 3 — the formula becomes a date

Mesopotamian years were named after royal deeds, and documents carry
those names as their dates. On a labor account from Girsu, the date
line runs the full titulary of king Šulgi — the formula you now know,
embedded in the middle of bookkeeping, opened by your new sign *mu*,
"year":

<figure class="reading reading--script">
  <div class="reading-lines">
    <div class="reading-line"><span class="script">𒈬 𒀭▢▢ ▢▢ 𒈗▢𒆠 𒈗𒀭▢▢▢▢▢ …</span><span class="translit atf">mu {d}szul-gi [nita] kal-ga# lugal uri5#{ki#} lugal an-ub-da limmu2-ba-ke4 ...</span><span class="gloss">Year: (when) Šulgi, mighty man, king of Ur, king of the four quarters, …</span></div>
  </div>
  <figcaption class="citation">
    Reverse iv 3′ of an Ur III account, Girsu.
    <a href="https://cdli.earth/artifacts/210013">CDLI P210013</a>
    via Nabu, <code>urn:nabu:cdli:p210013</code> (also carried, with
    gold lemmatization, as <code>urn:nabu:oracc:epsd2-admin-ur3:P210013</code>) ·
    license: attribution.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

The ATF spelling *szul-gi* is the same *š* you met as *še*: CDLI
writes *š* as *sz*, and its plain ASCII keeps index numbers full-size
(*uri5*, *ke4*) where this site prints them as subscripts (*uri₅*,
*ke₄*). One more convention and you have them all: the
prime (′) on the line number means the tablet's top is broken away,
so editors number from the first *surviving* line.

## Why the boxes are a promise

Every ▢ above is a sign the course still owes you. As your inventory
grows through this course and into the
[Sumerian track](../), boxes turn into signs — the same lines,
progressively unmasked. What the course will never do is show signs
out of order to fake completeness: <a href="{{ '/cuneiform/101/03-how-signs-mean/' | relative_url }}">chapter 03</a> called that the
"nothing untaught" rule, and it is enforced by the same build system
that publishes this page.

## Drill before you go on

1. From memory, write the four-line royal titulary of an Ur III king
   in transliteration, then check against Reading 1.
2. Write 𒈗 and 𒈬 ten times each, saying *lugal*, *mu*.
3. In Reading 3, underline every element you can parse: the silent
   determinatives, both *lugal* titles, the opening *mu*.
4. Explain to an imaginary student what [...] and # mean.
