## Where it comes from

*Tar* is "to cut" — and in Sumerian, deciding IS cutting: *nam
tar*, "to cut a destiny," is how the gods decree, and *di kud*
cuts verdicts in the same spirit. Fate, in this language, comes
off a blade.

## How to remember it

**Cut**: two wedges crossing like shears. When the great gods
sat down on the first day, what they did to the world's fates
was this sign — one clean decisive stroke each.

Piece by piece:

- **ud** — the day
- **nam kur-kur-ra** — the destiny of all the lands, *kur*
  doubled into a plural
- **ba-tar-ra** — was cut: the deciding blade, wrapped into "on
  which"

Destiny, cut once for every land at the beginning of things. The
formula is read in
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/17-the-day-destinies-were-decreed/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 17</a>.
