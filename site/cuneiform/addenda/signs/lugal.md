## Where it comes from

Not a picture but a composition — standard doctrine in the sign
lists: [LU₂]({{ '/cuneiform/addenda/signs/lu2/' | relative_url }})
"man" plus [GAL]({{ '/cuneiform/addenda/signs/gal/' | relative_url }})
"big" fused into one sign. The king is literally the **big man**.
In the classical form the two halves have melted together past
easy recognition, but the etymology stands.

## How to remember it

The **king** is the big man — the sign says so out loud. It is
also one of the heaviest signs you own, about a dozen wedges:
royalty takes work to write, which is easy to believe.

Piece by piece:

- **lugal** — king
- **me-en** — am I: the copula worn as an ending

A royal hymn opens with the king announcing himself, and a
librarian filed the whole poem under these three signs. The line
gives <a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/07-i-am-king/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 07</a>
its name.
