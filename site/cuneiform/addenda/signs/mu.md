## Where it comes from

Origin debated — an old suggestion sees a plant, but nothing worth
teaching as fact. What is certain is the double meaning: *mu* is
both "name" and "year," because Mesopotamian years WERE names —
each year named after a royal deed, and thousands of documents
dated by opening with this sign.

## How to remember it

Your **name**, written down, is what survives of you — and in
Sumer, each year's name was the one deed that survived of it. One
sign for both: what a thing is called is what it was.

Piece by piece:

- **mu** — "year (when …)": the formula's opening sign
- **{d}šul-gi** — the king's name under its divine star
- **kal-ga, lugal uri₅{ki}** — mighty, king of Ur: the titles the
  year drags along

A grain account, dated by naming the king — the working life of
this sign. The date formula is taught in
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/101/06-seals-and-bricks/' | relative_url }}">101's chapter 06</a>.
