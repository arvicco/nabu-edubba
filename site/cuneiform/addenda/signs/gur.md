## Where it comes from

Origin opaque — this sign is taught for its job, not its picture.
The *gur* was the great capacity measure of the grain accounts:
roughly 300 liters in the Ur III standard. When a granary counted
big, it counted in gur, and the measure-word follows the commodity
like a unit stamp: *n še gur*, "n gur of barley."

## How to remember it

The **bushel** basket at the granary door: everything pours
through it, nothing is official until it has. In an account line
it stands at the end like the stamp on a receipt — *…by the
bushel*.

Piece by piece:

- **2(u) še gur** — twenty gur of barley, the tens pressed into
  one cluster 𒎙
- **še ur₅-ra** — barley of the loan; *ur₅* is not yet yours
- **gur** — the measure stamped once more

Somebody owed the granary twenty bushels, and the tablet says so
twice. The measure is taught with the counting system in
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/101/05-counting/' | relative_url }}">101's chapter 05</a>.
