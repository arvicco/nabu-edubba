## Where it comes from

Commonly connected with a reed mat — plaited rows are still
suggested by the wedges, though the late form is worn smooth. As
*kid* it stays a mat; as *lil₂* it is air, wind, spirit — the LIL
of *{d}en-lil₂*, Enlil, "Lord Wind" himself. And in the grammar it
serves as *ke₄*, the little tag that stacks a genitive and a doer
into one syllable.

## How to remember it

A woven mat is mostly **spirit**: air held in a lattice of reeds.
Wind you cannot draw — but you can draw the thing it passes
through. One humble mat, and it writes the name of the king of the
gods.

Piece by piece:

- **išib** — purification priest
- **an-na** — of An, the sky-god
- **-ke₄** — this sign, stacking the genitive's hidden /k/ with
  the doer's *-e*

The mat-sign, doing pure grammar at the end of a priest's title.
