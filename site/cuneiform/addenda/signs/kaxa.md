## Where it comes from

[KA]({{ '/cuneiform/addenda/signs/ka/' | relative_url }}) the
mouth with [A]({{ '/cuneiform/addenda/signs/a/' | relative_url }})
the water written inside it — the sign lists notate the
containment literally as KA×A. What is water doing in a mouth?
Being drunk: the compound reads *naŋ*, "to drink." A rare sign in
the counted corpora, taught because nothing shows the
containment move more clearly.

## How to remember it

**Drink**: a mouth with water already in it. The script does not
describe the act — it stages it, one sign swallowed by another.
Once you have seen the little streams inside the jaw, you cannot
unsee the gulp.

Piece by piece:

- **ga** — milk
- **naŋ-naŋ** — drink, drink — the mouth with water in it, twice

A child's name from one of the oldest name lists — milk and
drinking, doubled like a lullaby. Its twin,
[ga-gu₇-gu₇]({{ '/cuneiform/addenda/signs/kaxgar/' | relative_url }}),
eats instead; both are read in
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/02-the-case-that-hides/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 02</a>.
