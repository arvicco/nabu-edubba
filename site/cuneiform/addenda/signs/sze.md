## Where it comes from

An ear of barley on its stalk — a classic picture, and in the late
form you can still see it: a central stem with grain-wedges
hanging off in rows. Barley was Mesopotamia's staple, wage, and
currency, which is why this sign fills more account lines than
almost any other.

## How to remember it

**Barley** hangs heavy: a stalk with kernels stacked up both
sides, drawn the way a farmer would draw it. Wherever wealth is
being counted, look for the little ear of grain.

Piece by piece:

- **a gal-gal-e** — the great waters, doubled big, *-e* marking the
  doer
- **še** — with barley
- **si-si-a-da** — fill and fill, wrapped into a purpose: "so that
  they might"

The flood as a blessing: high water means full granaries. The line
is unpacked in
