## Where it comes from

An eight-pointed star — the classic picture, still legible in the
crossing wedges of the late form. It writes *an* "heaven" and the
sky-god An; by extension it reads *diŋir*, "god"; and placed
silently before a name it is the determinative *{d}*, the script's
little flag saying *a deity follows* — three jobs from one star,
taught in <a href="{{ '/cuneiform/101/03-how-signs-mean/' | relative_url }}">101's chapter 03</a>.

## How to remember it

**Heaven** is where the stars are: one star stands for all of it —
and for everyone who lives there. Whenever you see the star, look
up: sky, sky-god, or any god at all.

Piece by piece:

- **{d}** — the star, written but never spoken: *a goddess follows*
- **nin-hur-saŋ** — "Lady of the mountain slopes," her mountain
  sign still ahead of you

The first sign of a temple dedication, and no one ever pronounced
it. The whole inscription is read in
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/11-a-dedication-for-real/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 11</a>.
