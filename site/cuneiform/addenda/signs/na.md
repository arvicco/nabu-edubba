## Where it comes from

A workhorse syllabogram whose pictorial past is not transparent —
no story to teach. As a word it can be *na₄*-family "stone" in
some writings; as a syllable it is everywhere, above all inside
verb chains, where *-na-* says "for him, for her" — the dative
ring of the votive formula *mu-na-ru*, "dedicated it FOR him."

## How to remember it

A **stone** is what you find everywhere and build with anywhere —
this sign is the script's fieldstone, mortared into names, nouns,
and verb chains alike. Small, hard, indispensable.

Piece by piece:

- **ma-** — for me
- **du₃** — build
- **-na** — this sign closing the form; its exact grammatical
  shade here is the grammars' business, honestly deferred

A god asks twice for his temple, and the stone-syllable carries
the asking. The dream is read in
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/12-the-chain-completed/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 12</a>.
