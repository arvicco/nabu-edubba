## Where it comes from

The atomic vertical wedge — like [AŠ]({{ '/cuneiform/addenda/signs/asz/' | relative_url }})
it depicts nothing, because a counting stroke needs no picture.
This is the wedge of discrete things: one sheep, one worker, one
jar, repeated up to nine times in tidy rows. Later scribes also set
it silently before personal names, a little flag saying *a person
follows*.

## How to remember it

A **tally** mark, standing upright like the person it counts. Every
culture that ever counted on a doorpost or a prison wall drew
exactly this stroke; the scribes simply pressed theirs into clay.
One stands, one is counted.

Piece by piece:

- **1(diš)** — one person, one tally stroke
- **niŋ₂-{d}ba-ba₆** — her name, "Property-of-Baba"; the goddess
  Baba wears the divine star, and the name's last sign waits for a
  later course

One line of a ration list: a stroke, a name, a life in the
accounts. The same tablet's date formula closes
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/101/06-seals-and-bricks/' | relative_url }}">101's chapter 06</a>.
