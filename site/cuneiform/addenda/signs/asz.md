## Where it comes from

There is no picture behind this sign, and none was ever needed: it
is the atomic horizontal wedge itself, one press of the stylus
corner, the smallest thing the script can do. In accounts it counts
*one* of things measured in bulk — a gur of barley, a shekel of
silver — while its vertical twin [DIŠ]({{ '/cuneiform/addenda/signs/disz/' | relative_url }})
counts one of things counted by the piece.

## How to remember it

**One** stroke. The first mark any scribe ever pressed into clay,
and the first sign you learned to write. If you can make one wedge,
you already own it — there is nothing else to hold.

Piece by piece:

- **1(aš@c)** — one unit of grain; ATF's *@c* marks the curved,
  archaic variant of this very wedge
- **1(barig)** — a smaller grain measure, its sign not yet taught
- **ur-{d}nanše** — the man being paid, Ur-Nanše

A personnel roster pays a man his grain, and the first thing on the
line is this sign doing its oldest job. You first read this line in
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/101/07-of-gods-and-men/' | relative_url }}">101's chapter 07</a>.
