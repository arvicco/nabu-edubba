## Where it comes from

Origin debated — no picture survives scrutiny. The double reading
is the real story: as *tur* it means "small"; as *dumu* it means
"child, son." One sign for both, because a child IS the small one
— a piece of logic still legible across four millennia. In the
filiation formula *X dumu Y*, "X son of Y," it identifies nearly
everyone the archives ever named.

## How to remember it

The **child** is the small one of the house — the sign is compact,
few wedges, small on purpose. When a name needs a father, this
little sign does the introducing: *son of*.

Piece by piece:

- **dumu** — son of
- **ma-šum** — the father's name
- **bahar₂** — his trade: potter, its sign still ahead of you

No surnames in Mesopotamia — a man is his name plus his father,
and this sign makes the join. The formula is taught in
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/101/07-of-gods-and-men/' | relative_url }}">101's chapter 07</a>.
