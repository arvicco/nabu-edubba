## Where it comes from

Debated, but commonly taken as a rain-cloud or storm sign. The
meanings hang together the way weather does: *im* is rain and
storm — and it is also clay, because clay is what rain and river
make of the land. The scribes' own word for their writing medium
is this sign: a tablet is a piece of weathered sky.

## How to remember it

A **storm** front: one long wedge of cloud with the slanting
strokes of rain driving off it. What the storm leaves behind is
mud — and mud, in Sumer, is what you write on.

Piece by piece:

- **ur** — the dog
- **i-im-di** — wanders about: the verb chain warmed up by *i-im-*,
  with this sign as the second step

syllable inside its verb. The proverb row is read in
