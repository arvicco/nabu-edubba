## Where it comes from

As a word it is a levee or ditch (stated) — the earthwork lines
of an irrigated country. Its rank comes from grammar: the bare
vowel *e* marks the agent in context and sits inside everyday
words like *za-e*, "you" — enough duty to make it the
third-commonest sign of the literary corpus.

## How to remember it

A **levee** is a long bank with cross-cuts — and the sign is
exactly that: a row of wedges banked against a line. Not to be
mistaken for its towering cousin
[É]({{ '/cuneiform/addenda/signs/e2/' | relative_url }}), the
house: the levee lies low, the house stands tall.

Piece by piece:

- **za-e** — you: this sign closing the pronoun
- **-me-en** — are: the copula
- **en / lugal** — lord, king: the two claims

Inside "you," inside the agent's tag, inside half the corpus —
the levee-sign is everywhere water is. The line is read in
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/13-you-are/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 13</a>.
