## Where it comes from

An arrow — and the textbook rebus pair of the whole discipline:
*ti* "arrow" and *ti(l)* "life" sounded close enough in Sumerian
that the arrow sign was drafted to write "to live." Every
dedication "for his life" fires this arrow. It also serves in
the receipt verb *šu … ti*, "to receive" — literally to make
something approach the hand.

## How to remember it

An **arrow** in flight is the fastest thing a Sumerian ever saw
— and life is the thing that flies. Four wedges, fletching and
point; aim it at *nam-* and you get Life itself.

Piece by piece:

- **nam-ti** — life: the arrow inside the abstraction
- **-la-ni-še₃** — his … for: glue, possessor, purpose-rope

The most repeated purpose in Sumerian building texts, and an
arrow carries it. The rebus is taught in
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/04-the-verbal-chain/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 04</a>.
