## Where it comes from

Three mountain peaks — a classic picture, kept through every
rewriting of the script as three simple wedges. Sumer itself is
river flatland; mountains stood on the horizon where the foreigners
lived, so one picture came to mean *mountain*, *highland*, and
*foreign land* at once. Doubled as *kur-kur*, it means "all the
lands."

## How to remember it

A **mountain** range needs three peaks — count the wedges: one,
two, three, a child's drawing of a skyline. Where the flat land
ends, the *kur* begins.

Piece by piece:

- **en zid** — true lord
- **a** — seed, literally "water"
- **kur gal-e** — by the Great Mountain (Enlil's own epithet), the
  *-e* marking the doer
- **ri-a** — poured out, the *-a* wrapping the verb into a
  description

Enlil himself is *kur gal*, the Great Mountain. The line is walked
through fully in
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/10-in-those-days/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 10</a>.
