## Where it comes from

A horn in profile — the ox's curve straightened into wedges. As
a verb, *si* is "to fill": doubled, *si-si*, it is what floods
do to fields and harvests do to granaries. A horn is also the
oldest cup — the filling and the vessel share a shape.

## How to remember it

A **horn** fills from the point up: tip it and pour. One horn is
the thing; say it twice and everything brims — *si-si*, full to
the rim.

Piece by piece:

- **a gal-gal-e** — the great waters, as doer
- **še** — with barley
- **si-si-a-da** — so that they might fill and fill: the horn
  doubled into abundance

High water, full granaries — the blessing the temple was built
to secure. The line is read in
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/14-a-row-of-proverbs/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 14</a>.
