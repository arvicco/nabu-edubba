## Where it comes from

Two wedges — the simplest sign of the Sumerian course, carrying
one of Sumer's deepest words. The *me* are the divine ordinances,
the powers that make civilization run: kingship, crafts, truth,
lament. The same sound also serves the copula: *-me-en*, "I am /
you are," which puts this little sign inside every royal boast.

## How to remember it

The **rites** are the smallest signs with the largest reach — two
strokes that hold up the world, the way ritual does. When a king
says *lugal-me-en*, "I AM king," the being-verb he leans on is
this sign.

Piece by piece:

- **lugal-me-en** — king am I
- **šag₄-ta** — from the womb, literally "from the heart"
- **ur-saŋ-me-en** — hero am I: the copula again

Twice in one line, the rites-sign does the *am*. The hymn is read
in <a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/15-i-am-the-king/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 15</a>.
