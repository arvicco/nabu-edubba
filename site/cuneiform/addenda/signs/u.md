## Where it comes from

The third of cuneiform's three elementary strokes: the corner
wedge, or *Winkelhaken* — not a drawn line but the stylus **tip**
pressed straight down into the clay, leaving a hooked triangular
dent. It depicts nothing; its job is number. One press is ten;
five presses count fifty.

## How to remember it

**Ten** is a corner: when your tallies reach ten, you stop drawing
strokes and stamp a corner instead — the counting hand folding a
full set into one dent. Tallies stand in rows;
tens sit as hooks.

Piece by piece:

- **šunigin** — "total"; its compound sign is not yet yours
- **2(u) 8(diš)** — twenty-eight: in this scribe's tight hand the
  two ten-corners press into one cluster 𒎙, and the eight tallies
  into 𒐍
- **kir₁₁ / sila₄-nita₂** — female lamb, male lamb; the herding
  vocabulary waits for a later course

A shepherd's year, closed out in corners and strokes. The counting
system itself is taught in
