## Where it comes from

[SAG]({{ '/cuneiform/addenda/signs/sag/' | relative_url }}), the
head, with extra strokes hatched across the jaw to mark the mouth
— the sign lists call this move *gunû*, "decorated." A classic
formation: take a picture, mark the part you mean. It writes *ka*,
"mouth," and hosts the script's boldest trick — writing another
sign INSIDE the mouth to say what the mouth is doing.

## How to remember it

A head, but look at the **mouth**: the hatching sits right where
the lips are, like a speech-bubble scribbled onto a face. Feed it
[water]({{ '/cuneiform/addenda/signs/kaxa/' | relative_url }}) and
it drinks; feed it a
[portion]({{ '/cuneiform/addenda/signs/kaxgar/' | relative_url }})
and it eats.

Piece by piece:

- **kalam** — the Land, Sumer itself
- **-ma-** — the genitive bridge, *kalam-ma* "of the Land"
- **-ka** — this sign: the genitive's hidden /k/ written out
  because a vowel follows

The mouth-sign moonlighting as pure grammar — no eating involved.
The unmasking of that /k/ is
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/07-i-am-king/' | relative_url }}">102 chapter 07's</a>
