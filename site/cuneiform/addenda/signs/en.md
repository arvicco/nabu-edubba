## Where it comes from

Origin opaque — the late form gives no picture to hold. The word
carries the weight instead: *en* is "lord," and in the oldest
cities also the high priest, the one who stood for the city before
its god. It ranks among the most frequent signs of the literary
corpus, because hymns are always addressing someone above.

## How to remember it

The **lord** holds the middle of the sign like a seat — a frame
with the bar of authority across it. Where prayers and epics need
a superior — god, king, master — this sign opens the address.

Piece by piece:

- **en** — lord
- **za-e-me-en** — you are: *za-e* "you" wearing the copula
- **lugal za-e-me-en** — the same claim, doubled up to king

Praise says it twice — Sumerian's oldest intensifier. The line is
read in
