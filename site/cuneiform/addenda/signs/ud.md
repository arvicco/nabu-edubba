## Where it comes from

Commonly explained as the sun rising between hills, though the late
form keeps only a hint of it. It writes *ud*, "day" — and by the
same picture, *utu*, the sun and the sun-god Utu. Day, sun, and the
god of both: one sunrise does all three.

## How to remember it

**Day** breaks where the sun clears the ridge — a big wedge of
light pushing over the horizon line. When the texts want "on that
day," "in those days," "daylight," this is the sign that rises.

Piece by piece:

- **ud** — day
- **re** — that (distant) one
- **-a** — the locative "in"

"In that distant day" is how Sumerian literature says *once upon a
time* — whole poems were filed by this opening. It anchors
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/10-in-those-days/' | relative_url }}">102's chapter 10</a>.
