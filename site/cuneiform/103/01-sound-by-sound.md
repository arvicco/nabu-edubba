# Spelling sound by sound

Sumerian gave every sign a syllable-sound, and Akkadian's whole
writing system is that one fact, used relentlessly. A sign that
sounds like *li* can spell the *li* in any word that needs one —
no meaning attached, pure sound. The field calls a sign on this
duty a syllabogram, and this chapter is about the craft of reading
them in runs.

## Two kinds of building block

Nearly every sign you will meet on syllable duty is one of two
shapes: **consonant + vowel** (*ša, li, ma, šu*) or **vowel +
consonant** (*um, iš, ir*). Akkadian words chain them so that the
signs overlap at the joints: a closed syllable like *lim* can be
spelled *li-im*, the vowel written twice on purpose. Watch the
system carry a whole word — the possessive ending *-šu*, "his,"
riding on the noun *eli*, "over":

The pattern to internalize: **read the signs aloud, in order, and
let the word assemble in your ear.** Akkadian spelling is built
for the voice; a reader who mutters is doing it correctly.

## The endings that follow you home

Two small endings from this page's words will recur so often that
they are worth naming now: **-šu**, "his/its" (masculine), and
**-ša**, "her/its" (feminine). *Elīšu*, "against him"; *išdāša*,
"its (own) foundations." Akkadian hangs its possessors on the back
of the noun, and once you can spot the ending, the rest of the
word is suddenly shorter.

## Drill before you go on

1. Write 𒇷, 𒅖, 𒅕 from memory; say <em>li</em>, <em>iš</em>,
   <em>ir</em>.
2. Spell <em>e-li-šu</em> in signs, unaided. What does the -šu
   contribute?
3. <em>iš-da-ša</em> — which sign is the possessive, and whose
   foundations are they?
4. Say why Akkadian needs vowel-consonant signs like IŠ when
   Sumerian could mostly live without leaning on them. One
   sentence.
