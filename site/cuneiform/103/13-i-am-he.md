# I am he

Since
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/103/04-when-lofty-an/' | relative_url }}">chapter 04</a>
this course has promised you the moment the king stops speaking
in the third person. It is here — one word,
<span class="norm">anāku</span>, "I" — and on the way to it, the
stele pays off two more debts: the blessing that echoes the
creed of
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/103/07-the-strong-and-the-weak/' | relative_url }}">chapter 07</a>,
and the verb
<a href="{{ '/cuneiform/103/08-more-than-one/' | relative_url }}">chapter 08</a>
left hanging in the air.

Item by item — three new pieces, and one long-awaited word:

- <span class="norm">liṭīb</span> — "may he make well": ṭâbum,
  the well-verb of
  <a href="{{ '/cuneiform/103/07-the-strong-and-the-weak/' | relative_url }}">chapter 07's</a>
  ṭubbim, wearing l- in front — the **precative**, the wish
  form. One letter turns a statement into a blessing. Note the
  echo: the commission said *ana šīr nišī ṭubbim*; the epilogue
  answers *šīr nišīšu liṭīb*. The monument rhymes with itself.
- <span class="norm">ša … išrukūšum</span> — "to whom (Šamaš)
  granted":
  <a href="{{ '/cuneiform/103/00-orientation/' | relative_url }}">chapter 00's</a>
  ša opening a clause, the granting-verb
  carrying -šum, "to him" — the giving family of suffixes, one
  vowel from -šu.
- <span class="norm">anāku</span> — "I": the independent
  pronoun, standing at the end of eleven lines of titles like a
  signature at the end of a page. Everything before it — king
  of justice, favorite of Šamaš — is one long noun phrase, and
  anāku is the verbless sentence that owns it:
  *all of that — am I.*
- <span class="norm">ul išâ</span> — "have no (rival)": the
  having-verb, negated with this chapter's ul.
  <a href="{{ '/cuneiform/103/05-the-verb-arrives/' | relative_url }}">Chapter 05's</a>
  lā lives inside conditions and wishes; **ul** is for flat
  statements of fact. The king's boast could take no other
  negation.

## Drill before you go on

1. Write 𒌌 from memory; say <em>ul</em>.
2. Turn a verdict into a blessing: what did the l- do to
   <span class="norm">ṭâbum</span>? Say both forms aloud.
3. lā or ul: "he did not convict him" — "my deeds have no
   rival" — "so that the strong not wrong the weak." Answer,
   then say why.
4. Recite the six title-lines from
   <span class="norm">Ḫammurapi</span> to
   <span class="norm">anāku</span>, aloud, from the signs.
