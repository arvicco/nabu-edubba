# An eye for an eye

Everyone has heard this law. Almost no one has read it. It sits
two hundred laws deep in the stele, four lines long, and every
word of it is about to be yours.

Item by item — the famous line runs on everything you own:

- <span class="norm">īn</span> — "the eye of": the eye-WORD,
  <span class="norm">īnum</span>, in
  <a href="{{ '/cuneiform/103/03-lord-of-heaven-and-earth/' | relative_url }}">chapter 03's</a>
  construct state — and spelled i-in, sound by sound, while the
  eye-SIGN
  <a href="{{ '/cuneiform/102/06-names-that-are-sentences/#sign-12146' | relative_url }}">IGI</a>
  sits this law out. The scribe had a picture of an eye and
  chose the sounds instead.
- <span class="norm">mār</span> — "son of": the sumerogram
  DUMU, construct again — two constructs stacked: *the eye of
  the son of a man*.
- <span class="norm">uḫtappid</span> — "has blinded": the
  blinding-verb in the D stem with
  <a href="{{ '/cuneiform/103/09-the-stems-named/' | relative_url }}">chapter 09's</a>
  -t- infix — the deed done, decisive, on the record.
- <span class="norm">uḫappadū</span> — "THEY shall blind": the
  same D verb, durative, with
  <a href="{{ '/cuneiform/103/08-more-than-one/' | relative_url }}">chapter 08's</a>
  plural -ū — the court acts, not a person. The law answers a
  singular crime with a plural verdict.

Reassembled: "If a man has blinded the eye of a man's son — his
eye they shall blind." Symmetry as justice, one verb pointing
both ways.

## The law beside it

The very next ruling swaps the eye for a bone. Its three new
signs belong to a later batch, so read it as an exhibit — the
sumerogram in the middle is the skeleton's own compound,
GIR₃.PAD.RA₂, "bone":

Same skeleton as law 196 in every sense: perfect for the deed,
plural durative for the verdict, the -t- and the -ū doing
exactly what they did one law up. Read law 196 until it is
easy; law 197 then reads itself — which is the deepest fact
about the Codex: it is one grammar, applied two hundred and
eighty-two times.

   <em>ab</em>.
2. <span class="norm">uḫtappid</span> vs

   stem, tense, number, and who is acting.
3. Why is the eye spelled i-in and not written 𒅆? What does
   that tell you about how scribes chose?
4. Two constructs stand in a row in law 196. Unstack them.
